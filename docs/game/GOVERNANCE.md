# Governance and institutional scope

**Purpose:** define the minimum civic consequence in Riverhold and the boundary before deeper governance becomes justified.

**Status:** DECISION PROPOSED — scenario-scoped proof only

**Authority boundary:** this file owns offices, rules, collective decisions, and conflicts as product systems. [WORLD_MODEL](WORLD_MODEL.md) owns facts; [CHRONICLE](../product/CHRONICLE.md) owns narration; 001 excludes generalized governance.

**Related documents:** [game systems](GAME_SYSTEMS.md), [world structure](../product/WORLD_STRUCTURE.md), [progression](../product/PROGRESSION.md)

## Owned decision

The slice includes one **scenario-scoped council rule transition**, not a governance engine. Riverhold has a council identifier, a storekeeper role, a fixed petition threshold, a fixed vote fixture, and `public-reserve-counts` before/after state. Mara cannot directly legislate; her verified public act and endorsements trigger an authorized vote whose recorded result changes future ledger display/allocation input.

This supplies institutional memory: a personal relationship cost can coexist with a public rule consequence. It does not claim elections, constitutions, ideology, enforcement, crime, justice, diplomacy, religion, or war.

## Future contract after gates

Any generalized institution must have a charter, jurisdiction, eligibility, offices, decision procedure, resources, versioned rules, enforcement mechanism, appeal/change procedure, and event-grounded legitimacy history. A model may propose language but never interpret authority or write law directly. Public writes are bounded and moderated separately from factual state.

## Power and newcomer rule

Institutions must create roles and contestable decisions rather than permanently lock founders' advantage. A mature newcomer can contribute evidence, fill a vacancy, apprentice, petition, or challenge precedent. No paid status, model status, or out-of-world account privilege maps to civic power.

## Rejected alternatives

Reject generalized voting, constitutional text generation, autonomous legal interpretation, real-money markets/governance, player elections, law/religion/war depth, faction trees, propaganda feeds, and LLM government in 001. Reject calling the fixed Riverhold vote “emergent politics.”

## Reopen evidence

Generalize only if Gate B players care about the rule because of the people involved, can explain its causal path, and session-5/20 tests ask meaningful institution questions. Remove even the fixed rule if it feels like scripted decoration or consumes the proof budget.

## Remaining uncertainty

**PRODUCT HYPOTHESIS:** one institutional consequence makes the world feel larger than the focal citizen. **UNRESOLVED:** whether fixed vote mechanics are perceived as authentic, and what governance depth remains safe/legible for a solo product.

## Resulting implementation behavior

Implement only IDs/state/transitions required by RV-010 through RV-012, with tests and Chronicle provenance. No generic ballots, office UI, law editor, moderator console, or public governance.

## Constraint fit

A fixed rule fixture reuses typed events and adds no server, moderation operation, model, corpus, partnership, payment, regulated data, or enterprise burden. It is cuttable if 52-hour evidence is threatened.
