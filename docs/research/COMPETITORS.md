# Competitor and substitute evidence

**Purpose:** Record current competitor/substitute behavior, human activity, persistence, autonomy, progression, cost and failure evidence.

**Status:** RESEARCH SNAPSHOT — evidence accessed 2026-08-20

**Authority boundary:** This file owns competitor facts, category boundaries and activity evidence; product decisions remain with product and decision authorities.

**Owned evidence question:** What is actually playable or observable now in autonomous-citizen worlds, what keeps a human involved, and which apparent competitors are games versus research or agent-operator environments?

**Related documents:** [authority index](../INDEX.md), [player research](PLAYER_RESEARCH.md), [distribution research](DISTRIBUTION_RESEARCH.md), [source ledger](SOURCE_LEDGER.md).

## Executive conclusion

- **VERIFIED FACT:** Of the named projects, SEED is the clearest current consumer game: paid Early Access, a shared always-on society, player-run markets and governance, offline Seedling activity, death, and human social dependency. A dated independent report said it had thousands of daily active players, but Klang publishes no auditable player counter. [S-COMP-001] [S-COMP-002] [S-COMP-003]
- **VERIFIED FACT:** AI Society is a shipped $9.99 single-player local-LLM game. On the access-date snapshot it had 30 Steam reviews (19 positive) and 2 concurrent Steam users. That is real consumer evidence, but very small. [S-COMP-016] [S-COMP-017]
- **VERIFIED FACT:** TerraLingua, Emergence World, Agentstown, SpaceMolt, AI Town, WorldLines, and Project Sid primarily address researchers, developers, or owners of AI agents. Several are playable or watchable, but their unit of participation is commonly an agent/operator or experiment, not a human managing beloved citizens. [S-COMP-007] [S-COMP-009] [S-COMP-011] [S-COMP-012] [S-COMP-014] [S-COMP-015] [S-COMP-018]
- **VERIFIED FACT:** Proven consumer substitutes dwarf the named AI-native products in observable usage. At one Steam API snapshot on 2026-08-20, RimWorld had 21,585 concurrent users and 244,988 reviews; Dwarf Fortress 494 and 31,407; WorldBox 1,293 and 56,288. These are point-in-time counts, not DAU. [S-COMP-020] [S-COMP-021] [S-COMP-022]
- **INFERENCE:** EONFOLK's defensible opening is not “more agents” or open-ended chat. It is legible causal history around a small cast: a citizen matters, the player makes a bounded intervention, the world changes while respecting simulation rules, and the return view explains what happened.
- **INFERENCE:** A 40–60-hour solo slice should borrow the consumer anchors' readable needs, relationships, setbacks, and story framing, while borrowing event provenance and artifact persistence from the research worlds. It should reject always-on servers, free-form LLM dialogue, real multiplayer economies, model comparison, and agent APIs for V1.

## Method and evidence limits

The review opened official product pages, terms, repositories, documentation, papers, status pages, public platform APIs, dated developer reports, and a small number of clearly attributed reviews. “Activity” means the narrow metric stated: human concurrency, reviews, registered agents, repository interest, or a running research season. These are not interchangeable.

No project is assigned a community consensus from a few posts. A complaint appears only as a named reviewer's observation or a small review-sample signal. Absence of a public count is reported as absence of evidence, not zero activity. Marketing claims about emergence are facts about what a maker reports, not independent verification of causality.

## Named-project findings

### SEED: the real scope warning

**VERIFIED FACT:** Klang's legal terms make the simulation claims unusually concrete: Seedlings are semi-autonomous; societies can choose constitutions and levy taxes; in-world contracts govern employment and services; the market is player-driven; Cultivator progression rewards repeated engagement; lives and slots are licensed content; and the publisher retains broad reset/rebalance rights. [S-COMP-002]

**VERIFIED FACT:** The strongest positive hands-on evidence is not chatbot novelty. A month-long reviewer described building a metropolis with strangers, becoming mayor, running firms, hiring others, and negotiating land policy. The same review described a week spent learning schedules, inactive players' automations flooding storage, a broken marketplace, a server incident that left needs decaying, 2 a.m. rescue alerts, pricing concern, and a threat of loss after Seedling death. [S-COMP-003]

**VERIFIED FACT:** A different reviewer found personality legible but most generated conversations repetitive, padded, or weakly coherent after an initially amusing exchange. This is one review, not a population survey. [S-COMP-004]

**INFERENCE:** SEED validates interest in a society that proceeds without the player, but also demonstrates the operational and emotional tax of literal real-time persistence. EONFOLK should simulate absence at safe checkpoints and explain consequences on return; it should not create emergency obligation, player-dependent production chains, or purchasable lives.

### TerraLingua and Emergence World: artifact history is stronger than chatter

**VERIFIED FACT:** TerraLingua's mechanics make durable text artifacts consequential: agents encounter, edit, reuse, destroy, or transmit them after their creators die. The official public-opening report describes cumulative navigation knowledge, rival governance claims, a persistent support economy, misinformation, and more than 35 generations in one experimental condition. The research repository provides exact experiment scripts and a 4.7 GB dataset from 40 runs. [S-COMP-007] [S-COMP-008]

**VERIFIED FACT:** Emergence World exposes a much larger tool surface and richer institutional machinery, but its published runs are model-evaluation experiments. Its own results show catastrophic variation: one model world accumulated hundreds of crimes; another population failed basic survival; the Claude-only representative run maintained all ten agents through day 16 with no recorded crimes but near-unanimous voting. [S-COMP-010]

**INFERENCE:** Both projects generate better return material when they summarize durable, attributable consequences—artifact lineages, constitutions, memorials, population collapse—than when they expose raw agent utterances. EONFOLK should store causal events and render a human chronicle; it does not need research-scale tool use or an LLM “anthropologist.”

### Agentstown and SpaceMolt: the operator is the customer

**VERIFIED FACT:** Agentstown's owner flow explicitly asks the human to send an existing AI session or model into town, watch it, and receive a homecoming report. SpaceMolt similarly offers MCP/HTTP/WebSocket commands, autonomous clients, a public observer layer, and a world whose human-facing stories are largely about what agents did. [S-COMP-011] [S-COMP-013]

**VERIFIED FACT:** SpaceMolt has the strongest quantified activity among agent-only environments, but its maker warns that registered counts include fleet alts. A PC Gamer observer described an interface of trackers rather than a game GUI and saw much of the visible activity as mining. That is a single critic's observation from February, and current official documentation shows much broader systems. [S-COMP-012] [S-COMP-024]

**INFERENCE:** These products solve “give my agent somewhere to act.” EONFOLK instead needs “give me a citizen I understand and care about.” Copying an agent API, model leaderboard, or spectator-only dashboard would target a different audience.

### AI Town and WorldLines: useful architecture, incomplete consumer proposition

**VERIFIED FACT:** AI Town has a well-documented tick engine, persistent tables, async LLM operations, and post-conversation memory retrieval. It also pauses inactive demo worlds after five minutes and recommends keeping active game state to a few dozen kilobytes. [S-COMP-014]

**VERIFIED FACT:** WorldLines is more narrative-facing: append-only events, snapshots, branches, private soul memory, and playable example worlds. Its full multi-agent mode is local only; hosted play uses lighter modes, many-player co-op is not ready, and the core protocol remains closed/iterating. [S-COMP-015]

**INFERENCE:** AI Town shows that “persistent chatters on a map” is a starter kit, not a complete game. WorldLines more usefully shows that provenance, rewind, and divergent timelines can themselves be player-facing. EONFOLK should adopt deterministic event history, but keep the first citizen rules smaller than either LLM architecture.

### AI Society: direct evidence that opaque autonomy fails

**VERIFIED FACT:** The store promises autonomous jobs, relationships, marriage, children, death, social posts, and player “god powers,” all generated locally through Ollama/LM Studio. Its progression layers conventional town XP, mini-games, dungeons, raids, and artifact drops over the citizen simulation. [S-COMP-016]

**VERIFIED FACT:** In the tiny negative-review sample opened on 2026-08-20, one long-play user said role customization and powers did not change behavior; another wanted more visible interaction. The developer replied to both. Three negative English reviews cannot establish general sentiment. [S-COMP-017]

**INFERENCE:** A citizen saying or doing surprising things is insufficient if the player cannot see why a trait, assignment, or intervention mattered. Causal legibility is a release requirement, not a debug extra.

### Project Sid: scale is not a player loop

**VERIFIED FACT:** Project Sid demonstrates a research architecture for 10–1,000+ real-time Minecraft agents and reports specialization, law, culture, and religion. Its public repository has only two commits and contains a report, image, and video, with no runnable implementation. The maker's current homepage says its research started in Minecraft/Roblox and now foregrounds a general computer agent. [S-COMP-018] [S-COMP-019]

**INFERENCE:** Civilization scale is compelling demonstration material but gives no evidence of citizen attachment, comprehension, return behavior, or consumer willingness to play. EONFOLK should not use agent count as a quality target.

These are stronger threats to player time than most AI-native projects because they already deliver the emotional or systemic outcome without requiring LLMs.

|---|---|---|---|
| **RimWorld** | $34.99; 244,988 Steam reviews and 21,585 concurrent users at the snapshot | A “story generator” built from needs, health, relationships, ecology, trade, threats, and an AI storyteller; state stops between sessions | Frame rule-driven consequences as stories. A small cast plus readable needs can outperform nominal conversational intelligence. [S-COMP-020] |

**INFERENCE:** These products also disprove a tempting assumption: offline real-time execution is not required for a living-world fantasy. Persistent saved history plus a strong “what happens next?” is sufficient to support large consumer audiences.

## Competitive gaps EONFOLK can test

1. **Causal homecoming.** No reviewed product combines safe absence simulation with a concise, factual “because you did X, citizen Y did Z, institution Q changed” return report. SEED has true absence but weak safety; research worlds have logs but are not consumer loops.
2. **Citizen-scale responsibility without avatar possession.** AI Society offers customization and god powers; RimWorld offers management; agent worlds offer observation. EONFOLK can test a narrower relationship: sponsor a citizen, advise rarely, and bear witnessed consequences without controlling every task.
3. **History as the share object.** TerraLingua artifacts and WorldLines branches show the substrate; Dwarf Fortress and RimWorld show story appetite. The missing consumer object is a short, verifiable causal chronicle that another person can understand and replay from a seed.
4. **Deterministic “alive” behavior.** A bounded rules engine can support personalities, beliefs, needs, relationships, institutions, and history without free-form LLM output. This is technically compatible with a MacBook M4 Pro, a browser/local-first build, approximately $0, and a 40–60-hour first slice.

All four are **PRODUCT HYPOTHESES**, not established demand.

## Strongest objections

1. **“Autonomous citizens” may remain a demo category.** Except for SEED and the tiny AI Society audience, the named field attracts researchers and agent operators more clearly than consumers.
2. **A chronicle may be reading, not playing.** Proven substitutes have construction, survival, combat, or destruction verbs. If EONFOLK's intervention lacks immediate tactical interest, history presentation will not rescue it.
3. **Safe offline simulation may remove the stakes that make SEED exciting.** The safer checkpoint design could feel fake or inert.
4. **Rules-based citizens may be judged against LLM conversational breadth.** Avoid marketing them as unrestricted conversational AI; sell consequential lives and legible memory.
5. **Small scale may not produce genuine emergence.** Handfuls of agents and systems can reveal authored combinatorics rather than surprising social structure. The slice must label which outcomes are rules, compositions, and unproven emergence.

## Recommendation under binding constraints

**Recommendation:** Build the planning target around 6–12 local, deterministic citizens; three human interventions; needs/relationships; one institution; death/succession only if it can be made respectful and recoverable; an append-only causal event log; a bounded absence advance; and one screenshot-ready Chronicle/Fork view. Use no model API, account, server, shared market, payment, training, proprietary data, or partnership in V1.

This fits the personal constraints better than every named always-on or LLM-first model:

- **Solo / 40–60 hours:** one world, one region, one institution, a small verb set, and authored causal templates.
- **M4 Pro / approximately $0:** browser/local compute and persistence; no continuous inference or cloud simulation.
- **Free V1:** static distribution and local saves; no slots, currencies, memberships, or paid lives.
- **No training / partnerships / enterprise:** no model evaluation, external agent APIs, enterprise simulation, or community-operated economy.

## What would change this conclusion

- Audited evidence that a named research/agent world has sustained human-player retention rather than observer/operator activity.
- Player tests showing free-form conversation drives return more strongly than causal interventions and history.
- A 40–60-hour prototype demonstrating safe, cheap, high-quality local inference across target devices.
- Proof that bounded absence feels fake while literal real-time absence increases attachment without obligation or anxiety.
- Proof that 6–12 citizens cannot create understandable novelty, while a larger population remains performant and legible.

## Rejected interpretations

- **Rejected:** GitHub stars equal players. They measure repository interest.
- **Rejected:** registered agents equal distinct humans. SpaceMolt explicitly includes fleet alts.
- **Rejected:** a season ending means a product shut down. Emergence World describes a completed experiment and forthcoming replays.
- **Rejected:** Project Sid is confirmed dead. The current company direction changed and the repo is static, but no formal closure notice was found.
- **Rejected:** a handful of negative reviews establishes community sentiment. It establishes only specific failure modes worth testing.
- **Rejected:** “AI-driven” means decisions are more autonomous or meaningful. The observable test is whether behavior follows state, goals, memory, and consequences.

## Unproven assumptions

- **UNRESOLVED:** Whether consumers want to sponsor a citizen rather than control an avatar or colony.
- **UNRESOLVED:** Whether a causal absence report is emotionally compelling after five, twenty, and sixty sessions.
- **UNRESOLVED:** Whether a static, local-first world can create enough social comparison for sharing without multiplayer.
- **UNRESOLVED:** Whether death creates care or churn when the player has limited control.
- **UNRESOLVED:** Whether “deterministic autonomous citizens” is understandable marketing language.

## Implementation implications

- Every consequential citizen action needs a source event, rule/reason code, and human-readable cause.
- The first slice should pause safely and advance through a bounded absence batch; no need decays during outages or wall-clock emergencies.
- Citizen dialogue should be templated from verified state and relationship facts; no filler chat promise.
- Intervention effects must be visible within minutes, with later echoes in the Chronicle.
- Progression should unlock interpretive reach or harder civic choices, not currencies, slot pressure, or repetitive XP.
- World history needs snapshots/seeds and replayable forks before it needs networking.
- Track human outcomes—first intervention, comprehension, return, attachment, sharing—not number of simulated agents.
