# Gameplay

EONFOLK asks the player to care about one autonomous person inside a settlement
that continues without direct orders.

## Current loop

1. Read the physical world: people move, gather, carry, repair, exchange, and
   meet in recognizable places.
2. Select a citizen or place to understand the current activity and tension.
3. Sponsor Mara, learning her values, relationships, known facts, beliefs, and
   current Standing Plan.
4. Offer one high-level intervention—or explicitly abstain.
5. Mara independently accepts, rejects, delays, or reinterprets it.
6. Observe a later resource, relationship, belief, or civic consequence.
7. Return after time away and inspect a replayable, factual Chronicle.

The player cannot assign routine jobs or force a desired outcome. Counsel is
rare because the interesting decision is whether and when to risk influence.

## Simulation surface

The current product proof contains:

- eight citizens;
- food, water, and wood as conserved integer resources;
- hunger, thirst, rest, and bounded social pressure;
- gathering and consumption;
- one atomic bilateral exchange;
- one simple wood-and-work repair loop;
- public and private typed communication;
- sourced beliefs, relationships, reputation markers, and Standing Plans; and
- one settlement consequence linked to the sponsor decision.

These are understandable primitives, not a generalized economy or story
scripting framework. A deterministic Standard Brain handles routine behavior,
so every required loop works with all model integrations removed.

## The first sponsor decision

Mara is acting on a thin water store, and Iven is the friend who shares that worry. The
player may advise checking the stores first, raising the shortage with Iven, or abstain.
Mara evaluates that input against her evidence, relationships, values, and
existing plan. Different choices can preserve or strain trust and change what is
verified. No generated Dawnmere record currently supports a Mara–Toma theft or
ledger story; that chain remains frozen Riverhold evidence.

The Chronicle is careful about attribution. Advice can contribute to a plan;
Mara's statement can trigger an audit; an audit can verify a reserve. None of
those facts proves theft, moral intent, or that the player personally caused a
later rule.

## What is deliberately absent

There is no unrestricted dialogue, combat, reproduction, broad law/religion/war
simulation, currency market, technology tree, multiplayer, account, payment,
hosted inference requirement, or browser-model download.

The fastest falsification question is not whether the architecture can scale.
It is whether players care about Mara after ten minutes and return because her
unresolved relationship and settlement consequence matter.
